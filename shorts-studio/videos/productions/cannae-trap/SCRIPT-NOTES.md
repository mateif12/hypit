## Fact basis (Battle of Cannae, 216 BC). Please verify if accuracy is critical.

- **Roman strength ~86,000:** Polybius gives 80,000 infantry + about 6,000 cavalry. Modern estimates
  are often lower, so "eighty-six thousand" follows the ancient account.
- **Hannibal ~50,000:** Polybius gives about 40,000 infantry + 10,000 cavalry.
- **"Just past thirty":** Hannibal is usually dated to 247 BC, which makes him about 30–31 at Cannae.
  The wording avoids a single exact age.
- **"Two Roman armies":** the Trebia (218 BC) and Lake Trasimene (217 BC) defeats.
- **Tactic:**
  - Gallic and Spanish infantry formed a convex centre that gave ground.
  - African veteran infantry on the flanks turned inward.
  - Hasdrubal's cavalry routed the Roman cavalry, rode behind the Roman army and attacked from the rear.
- **Dead:** Polybius says about 70,000; Livy says about 48,000. Hence "up to seventy thousand"; the
  battle was fought in one day.
- **Original wording:** none of the reference's sentences are reused. Only the structural pattern is shared.
